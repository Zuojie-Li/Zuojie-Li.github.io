# Abstract
In clinical practice of prostate cancer (PCa) management, the high renal uptake of prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted radiotracers remains a significant concern. To address this challenge, the study systematically evaluated molecular modification strategiestargeting moieties, linker types, and coligand compositionsfor their effects on tracer pharmacokinetics. Among candidates, [99mTc]Tc-EuK2-LPRO-HYNIC-EDDA was the most promising for single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) imaging. It binds PSMA stably with nanomolar affinity (Kd = 16.28 nM), and the high blocking efficiency in vitro and in vivo subsequently confirmed excellent targeting specificity. The tracer cleared rapidly from nontarget organs, effectively mitigating renal accumulation and giving high target-to-background ratios. Crucially, high-contrast SPECT/CT images obtained 2−6 h post-injection indicate clinical promise for routine and delayed PCa imaging. Additionally, density functional theory (DFT) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations provided critical theoretical insights into coordination structure, ligand−protein interaction mechanisms, and correlations between conformational differences and biological activity.  Radiolabeled human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) tracers are important tools for the accurate clinical diagnosis and treatment of HER2-positive tumors. The aim of this study was to investigate the potential of small molecule inhibitors (lapatinib) as target groups for novel radiotracers targeting HER2. Twelve stable radiotracers were prepared by chelation with different coligands. The relationships between the linker and the combination of coligands with pharmacodynamics were systematically evaluated. [<sup>99m</sup>Tc]Tc-Lapa-HYNIC-TPPTS exhibits the most favorable pharmacokinetic properties, as it can stably bind to the active pocket of the HER2 protein. Both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated its high targeting specificity toward HER2. SPECT images of [<sup>99m</sup>Tc]Tc-LapaHYNIC-TPPTS in HER2-positive model mice revealed that it can effectively locate tumors. This study is the first attempt at a <sup>99m</sup>Tc-labeled small-molecule inhibitor tracer targeting HER2, showing great potential for applications in the diagnosis of HER2-positive cancers.
